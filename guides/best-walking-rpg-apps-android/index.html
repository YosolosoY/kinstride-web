The one distinction that matters: GPS or steps

GPS-based apps place the game on your real streets. Monsters and dungeons show up near you. This makes the world feel real, but it needs location access and, in practice, more screen time or location services running.

Step-based apps read your phone’s pedometer or a health platform. The game world is separate from where you are, so it works on a treadmill, indoors, or on a route you have walked a hundred times.

Neither is better. It depends on whether you want your neighborhood to be the map or you want to escape it.

Notes on each

Orna: your streets are the map

Orna is the pick if you want a GPS game with classes, crafting and raid bosses. According to the MistyWay comparison, it is free with no ads. The trade-off is that you play it on your real surroundings, so it suits walks outdoors.

WalkScape: skills by steps

Per the same source, WalkScape is step-based, works offline, and is Android-only and still in closed beta. That limits who can try it right now.

The Walk: a finite story

The Walk is a story with a beginning and an end: a courier carrying a detonator to Edinburgh over 65 episodes, according to the comparison. The first five episodes are free and the rest need a purchase or subscription. If you like a plot more than a stat sheet, this is a different kind of motivation.

Zombies, Run!: for runners

It is built around running, with GPS tracking and story missions. The comparison lists it at $6.99/month. A separate figure of $7.99 appeared in our research, so we are not sure which is current. Check the store.

Run the Realm

PocketGamer’s coverage of its Google Play re-release (January 2025) says jogging, running, cycling and walking all advance a fantasy story, with a mage, knight or thief class. It says nothing about tracking or pricing, so we cannot tell you either. The reviewer notes the AI-generated art can look muddy up close.

Fantasy Hike

Laughing Squid covered it in January 2024: your steps move a halfling avatar across a Lord of the Rings-themed map to Mount Fire. The article mentions Apple Health and Apple Watch, which suggests an iOS app, but it does not say so outright. We could not confirm Android support or pricing. That “walk to a faraway place” idea is popular, though.

How to choose

  • You walk mostly outdoors and like a live map: a GPS game such as Orna.
  • You walk on a treadmill, at the gym or on repeated routes: a step-based game.
  • You want a story with an ending: The Walk.
  • You run more than you walk: Zombies, Run!
  • You want a character you can also play at a table: see Kinstride below.
  • You hate subscriptions: read the store listing’s pricing section first. “Free” can still mean in-app purchases.

One more tip: test for a week before committing. Many people drop gamified apps after the novelty fades, and a week shows you whether the loop keeps pulling you out the door or only counts steps in the background.

Where Kinstride fits

Kinstride is an Android app we are building, and we make it, so weigh this section accordingly. Your real kilometers move you across a fantasy map, and the way you walk decides your character: your climbs, cadence, endurance and routine pick one of nine lineages and a class. Steps-to-map is the same basic idea as several apps above, so we do not claim it is new. We have not proven it keeps people walking longer, and we have no retention data to show you.

Meet the lineages: Halfling · Dwarf · Human · Elf · Orc · Barkfolk · Gnome · Goliath · Emberkin. The lineages page describes each one, and the test shows which fits how you walk.

Kinstride is free on Google Play, with no ads and no distance limit.

Use it at your table

We designed the character so it can sit at a role-playing table. Your character sheet has the layout a tabletop player expects: six abilities (Strength, Dexterity, Constitution, Intelligence, Wisdom and Charisma, each from 8 to 20), a modifier for each, armor class, hit points by class and level, and a proficiency bonus. The numbers are not rolled: they come from how you walk. Climbing hills raises Strength, a steady cadence raises Dexterity, long outings raise Constitution, camps found raise Intelligence, consistency raises Wisdom, and walking with company raises Charisma.

What this means in practice: bring your character to a game that uses the fifth-edition rules, show your game master the sheet, and ask whether the table accepts it. What it does not include yet: skills, saving throws, a backstory or spells, and the sheet is shared as an image rather than a file you can edit. Your abilities also recalculate as your walking changes, so a campaign would need you to agree with your game master on a fixed set of numbers.

The rules and names come from the SRD 5.2 (see the license notice at the end of this page). Kinstride is independent and has no affiliation with any tabletop publisher.
